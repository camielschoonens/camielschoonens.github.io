I recently added two new pieces to my photography gear, the Canon RF 15-35mm f/2.8L IS USM and the Benro Pro Filter Kit. Tonight I took both out for the first time, shooting in the polder landscape near Oudewater to practice working with the setup before going on summer holiday. The lens at the wide end at 15mm gives a sense of depth and space that works well in flat polder landscapes where the sky is often the most interesting element.

## The gear

The RF 15-35mm is a significant step up from the RF 16mm prime I have been shooting landscapes with. The Benro kit I bought includes a filter holder, a 2-stop graduated ND and a 3-stop graduated ND, plus a 6-stop solid ND. The system uses a slot-in holder with a gear-driven knob to precisely position the graduated filters. The system is expensive but the build quality is really good.

## What I learned

The filter system itself is well built and the gear knob for positioning the graduated filters is easy to use. That said, I still need a lot of practice to get the positioning right consistently.

One practical issue: the two graduated filters (2-stop and 3-stop) are nearly impossible to tell apart in the field. They are the same size and shape, and the difference in glass density is not visible to my eye. I need to find a way to tag or mark them so I can grab the right one without holding each up to the light.
